Session replay records the DOM as it changes, mouse movement, clicks, scrolls,
and rebuilds it as a video-like playback. It's not a screen recording; no
video is actually captured. PostHog stores a stream of DOM mutations and
replays them client-side when you watch a recording, which is why it stays
lightweight enough to run on every session by default.

Recording is already turned on for this project, and `posthog-js` starts
capturing it automatically as soon as it loads, no extra config needed beyond
the install itself.

## Try it: record yourself, then watch it back

1. Click around this site for thirty seconds or so. Visit a couple of guide
   pages, click a button, use the search.
2. Open [Session Replay](https://us.posthog.com/project/553726/replay).
   Recordings can take a minute or two to finish processing after the session
   ends, so if yours isn't there yet, give it a moment and refresh.
3. Find your session (sort by "Recent" if it's not already) and open it.
4. Watch the playback. Notice the event timeline running alongside the video:
   every `$pageview`, `$autocapture`, and custom event you fired shows up
   as a marker you can click to jump straight to that moment.

## What it's actually for

Replay earns its keep on sessions that went wrong: someone who rage-clicked a
dead button, filled out a form and abandoned it, or hit an error. PostHog
surfaces some of that automatically, filter the replay list by "rage clicks"
or "dead clicks" and you'll see sessions where a visitor clicked the same
spot repeatedly with nothing happening, usually a broken link, a JS error, or
a button that looks clickable but isn't.

This project also has two Replay Vision scanners running (LLM agents that
watch recordings on a schedule and flag what they find): one for visibly
broken pages under `/guide/`, one for rage-click sessions anywhere on the
site. Their findings, once they have any, land in the
[Self-driving inbox](https://us.posthog.com/project/553726/inbox) rather
than the replay list itself.
